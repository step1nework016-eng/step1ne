What is recruitment outsourcing? Permanent placement fees, process, and how it differs from headhunting

By:Step1ne Executive Search Consultant Team Published 2026-09-29 Last updated 2026-10-23 About 9 min read
Short answer

Outsourced recruitment (formally "commissioned recruitment" or "permanent placement") means a company pays a consulting firm to find, screen, and schedule interviews with candidates, and once hired, the employee is still the company's own full-time employee; the employer does not change. Fees are usually success-based, charged only when the candidate starts. At Step1ne, for example, the average is from 1.5 months' salary, depending on the difficulty of the role and the contract. Under Taiwanese regulations, there is a cap on what a recruitment agency can charge an employer; it cannot charge whatever it likes. It differs from headhunting mainly in the seniority targeted and how candidates are approached, and from staffing in "who the employer is."

1What is recruitment outsourcing? How it differs from headhunting and staffing

In Chinese, "代招" is short for "recruiting on someone's behalf." The formal industry term is commissioned recruitment (Direct Hire, a form of Recruitment Process Outsourcing): a company entrusts one or more permanent positions to a consulting firm, which finds candidates, does the initial screening and arranges interviews, while the company makes the final decision. Once the hire starts, the employer is the company itself; labor insurance, health insurance and the employment contract are all signed directly with the company. This is completely different from staffing (a dispatched worker's employer is the staffing agency, and the company is only the "client site"; see How staffing costs are calculated).

Contingency recruiting and executive search are often treated as the same thing, but more precisely, executive search is one type of recruiting service. It usually targets senior, confidential, or hard-to-fill roles (such as CEO or CFO), reaching out proactively under a confidential mandate to people who are not yet looking for a job. Recruiting services cover a wider range, from entry-level to mid- and senior-level roles, using database matching, direct outreach, and screening of regular applicants. For details on fee models and how to choose a firm, see How headhunter fees are calculated and How to choose a headhunting firm in Taiwan.

A simple rule of thumb: look at who the employer is to tell whether it's a recruitment agency/headhunter; look at the target level and approach method to tell whether it's an agency or a headhunter.

The legal pitfall companies most often hit with staffing: re-registering existing employees as dispatched workers

In May 2019 (ROC year 108), Taiwan's Labor Standards Act added Article 17-1, which explicitly prohibits the client company (the user enterprise) from interviewing and selecting a specific person first, then asking the staffing agency to put that worker on its payroll and dispatch them back—in other words, picking someone yourself and dressing it up as dispatch. If this is violated, the worker may, within 90 days of starting to provide services, notify the client company in writing of their intent to enter into an employment contract; if the client company fails to negotiate within the deadline or negotiations fail, an employment contract is deemed to exist from the day after the deadline (full text of Article 17-1 of Taiwan's Labor Standards Act). For the full penalties and compliance points, see Legal pitfalls when companies use dispatched workers.

Recruiting services and executive search don't run into the "re-registration" problem, because the hire is the company's own employee from day one. However, the fees that recruiting firms and headhunters charge companies are likewise regulated by Taiwan's Standards for Fee Items and Amounts Charged by Private Employment Services Institutions (see Section 2 below).

How to choose: a 3-question quick check

  1. Is this a long-term position, or short-term/replacement staffing? Long-term → outsourced recruitment or headhunting; short-term, seasonal, or leave cover → staffing
  2. Is the role a confidential senior position or a regular permanent role? Confidential senior roles such as CEO/CFO → headhunting; regular permanent roles → recruitment outsourcing
  3. Do you want to be the employer? Sign the employment contract and manage performance yourself → outsourced recruitment or headhunting; don't want to deal with insurance and payroll admin, just need flexible staffing → staffing

2How do recruiting agencies charge? Legal caps and fee structures

The most common fee model for outsourced recruitment is success-based: terms are agreed when you engage the firm, and you are charged only when a candidate actually starts; in principle, no hire means no fee. At Step1ne, for example, permanent placement averages from 1.5 months' salary, with the actual rate depending on the difficulty of the role and the engagement contract. We don't compare other firms' rates in this article; readers can request quotes from each firm directly.

What gets mentioned less often is that the fees an agency charges employers are actually capped by regulation. Under Article 3 of Taiwan's Standards for Fee Items and Amounts Charged by Private Employment Service Institutions, the registration and placement fees a for-profit employment service agency charges an employer may not, in total, exceed the hire's first month of salary if that salary is at or below the average wage published by the central competent authority; if it is above the average wage, the total may not exceed four months of the hire's salary. In addition, the service fee per employee may not exceed NT$2,000 per year (Laws & Regulations Database (Taiwan), Standards for Fee Items and Amounts Charged by Private Employment Service Institutions, Article 3). In other words, as long as a quote stays within the legal cap, the fee structure and amount are agreed in the contract between both parties; before you sign, you can ask the agency to explain the basis for its quote.

Note: This cap applies to fees charged to the "employer," which is separate from fees charged to the "job seeker." Under Article 4 of the same Standards, if a recruitment agency also charges the job seeker, the registration fee and placement fee combined may not exceed 5% of the job seeker's first month's salary (Article 4 of the same Standards). A legitimate recruitment service should state in the engagement contract who is charged and how much. Before signing, be sure to ask whether they charge only the company or the candidate as well.

3The full recruitment agency process: from engagement to start date

  1. Needs interview: confirm the job specs, requirements, salary range and hiring timeline, and write the fee terms into the engagement contract
  2. Sourcing and initial screening: matching against the talent database while proactively reaching out to suitable people who aren't applying yet. At Step1ne, AI conducts the first interview and a consultant then reviews it, cutting the time companies spend on unqualified résumés
  3. Submission: only candidates the consultant considers qualified and whose interest has been initially confirmed are sent, so in principle you can schedule interviews with them right away
  4. Interview scheduling and follow-up: coordinating both sides' calendars, checking both sides' interest after interviews, and handling salary negotiation and start dates
  5. Start date and billing: fees are charged only once the candidate officially starts; if they leave within the guarantee period, a free replacement is provided per the contract

The biggest difference from recruiting in-house is that the two most time-consuming tasks, "screening" and "scheduling interviews," are handed off; the company only interviews candidates who have already been screened once.

4When should you use recruitment outsourcing, and when not?

When recruitment outsourcing makes sense:

  • You need this person long-term, not for a short-term or seasonal need
  • HR is stretched thin, or you need to fill several openings at once
  • Hard-to-fill roles where public postings bring few applicants or uneven quality
  • Want guaranteed replacements so you don't start from zero each time
  • You need to reach passive candidates who aren't applying yet but fit the profile

When you don't necessarily need outsourced recruitment:

  • The position is attractive enough that public postings bring in plenty of qualified applicants
  • HR has time and the volume is small, so in-house hiring can handle it
  • If you need people for a short time or only temporarily, temporary staffing is usually the better fit. See How Staffing Costs Are Calculated
  • For high-volume, fast-turnover entry- and mid-level roles, see how the three approaches (in-house / outsourced recruitment / staffing) compare in Choosing recruitment outsourcing for entry- and mid-level roles

5Five questions to ask before choosing a recruitment agency

  1. Does it hold a valid employment services license? In Taiwan, private employment service agencies must by law obtain a license from the competent authority; you can simply ask for the license number and verify it
  2. When do fees start? Only when the hire starts, or is part charged upfront at signing?
  3. How long is the guarantee period? If the candidate leaves during the guarantee period, how is the replacement handled?
  4. Who screened candidates before they were sent? Are they just forwarding résumés, or has someone confirmed interest and qualifications by phone or interview first?
  5. How is progress reported? How often are updates sent, and can you see which stage each candidate is stuck at?

6Regulations recruitment agencies must follow

Key regulations
  • Fees have a legal cap: the registration and placement fees charged to an employer may not, in total, exceed one month's or four months' salary of the hire, depending on the salary level of the role (Article 3 of Taiwan's Standards for Fee Items and Amounts Charged by Private Employment Service Institutions)
  • Salary must be disclosed: for roles with regular pay below NT$40,000, the salary range must be published or disclosed to applicants (Article 5, Paragraph 2, Subparagraph 6 of Taiwan's Employment Service Act)
  • You can't screen candidates by age, gender, or similar criteria: this applies to outsourced recruitment too (Taiwan's Employment Service Act, Article 5, Paragraph 1). Requirements should be written as objective job conditions
  • Recruitment agencies must be properly licensed: providing private employment services requires a license from the competent authority, and operating without one is prohibited

7FAQ

Are outsourced recruitment and headhunting the same thing?+

Not exactly. Outsourced recruitment is the broader term, covering commissioned hiring at all levels; headhunting usually refers specifically to senior, confidential, or hard-to-fill roles, approaching passive candidates proactively under a confidential engagement. For details on fee models and how to choose, see How Are Headhunter Fees Calculated?.

Are recruitment outsourcing fees always paid only after the hire starts?+

Most firms charge on a success basis, billing only once the hire starts, but actual payment terms depend on the engagement contract both sides sign — confirm them before signing.

Is there a cap on recruitment outsourcing fees?+

Yes. Under Article 3 of Taiwan's "Standards for Fee Items and Amounts Charged by Private Employment Service Agencies," the registration and placement fees charged to an employer may not, combined, exceed the employee's first month's or four months' salary, depending on the salary level of the role, and there is also an annual cap on service fees per employee.

With outsourced recruitment, who is responsible for the hire's insurance and employment contract?+

They are the employees of the company that commissioned the recruitment. The recruitment firm only finds, screens, and schedules interviews; once the candidate starts, the employer is the company itself. This is where it differs from staffing.

Can a small company use a recruitment agency?+

Yes. Recruitment outsourcing isn't limited by company size — SMEs with limited HR capacity often need it more, to save time when a single role is hard to fill.
