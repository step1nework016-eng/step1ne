Southeast Asia overseas jobs: how do you tell real from fake?

By:Step1ne Executive Search Consultant Team Published 2026-07-27 About 6 min read
Short answer

⚠ The most important thing first

Job scams in Cambodia are not old news — they're happening now. In April 2026, Cambodian police raided a scam operation in a hotel in Phnom Penh; of the 311 foreign nationals arrested, 182 were Taiwanese.

So whenever you see a job in Cambodia (or elsewhere in Southeast Asia), the first step isn't weighing the pay—it's verifying whether it's real. The first half of this article covers how to check; only the second half discusses what legitimate overseas postings look like. Don't reverse the order.

The scam formula: when all five signs appear together, it's a red flag

Almost every overseas job scam follows the same template; if you can recognize it, you can avoid 90% of the risk:

  1. High pay + free meals and housing + no experience needed + no education required — legitimate jobs never offer all four at once. Work that truly requires no skills doesn't pay far above market
  2. Can't explain the job clearly: when asked what the work involves, they only say "customer service," "typing," or "online chat," and can't describe the process or responsibilities
  3. You've never met anyone from the company—all contact is through messaging apps, there's no formal interview, and you never meet the hiring manager
  4. They barely care about your abilities but hire you right away: normal hiring assesses fit; scams only care whether you'll actually show up
  5. Pressure to leave immediately — you're given very little time to think, and they may even buy your plane ticket and arrange airport pickup
⚠ Never agree to any of these

Handing over your passport, ID documents, credit cards or phone — this is the first step toward controlling your freedom of movement; legitimate employers don't ask for it.
Signing debt or loan documents. A common line is "we'll cover your flight and agency fee now and deduct it from your pay later" — once you sign, you're bound by debt.
Accepting group housing and shuttle transport with no freedom to go out, which is a precursor to de facto confinement.

If you or someone you know is already in trouble overseas, call the Emergency Contact Center of Taiwan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs at 0800-085-095 (from within Taiwan), or contact the 24-hour emergency line of Taiwan's local overseas mission.

How do you verify an overseas job is real?

Finish all five of these steps before deciding; there's no need to rush a few days:

  1. Check that the company actually exists: search its full name and confirm it has a website, news coverage, and traces of other employees on social media. If all there is is a single LINE account, rule it out
  2. Ask for the business registration number and employment services license number — in Taiwan, recruitment intermediaries must be licensed by the competent authority. Check the registration number in the Ministry of Economic Affairs' business registration lookup system to confirm the company is operating
  3. Confirm they won't charge you: legitimate recruitment agencies charge the employer. Under Taiwanese law, they may not withhold a job seeker's property or collect a placement guarantee deposit
  4. Insist on a formal interview, online or in person, where you can talk to someone from the hiring team and get answers on team size, who you report to and what the work actually involves
  5. Check the employment contract. Before you leave, there should be a written contract stating the job, pay, working hours, contract period, and arrangements for returning to Taiwan. If it's only a verbal promise, don't go
A headhunter's view

The easiest signal to spot: a legitimate hiring process "screens you"; a scam doesn't.

Legitimate overseas assignments usually have strict requirements: relevant industry experience, specific professional skills, sometimes even interview tests and second-round interviews. Because postings abroad are expensive, companies don't send just anyone. Conversely, if an overseas opportunity asks almost nothing of your background yet offers high pay, that alone doesn't add up.

So what does a legitimate Southeast Asia assignment actually look like?

Taiwanese companies have extensive manufacturing and service operations in Southeast Asia (Vietnam, Cambodia, the Philippines, Thailand and elsewhere), and legitimate overseas assignments do exist. Their characteristics are the exact opposite of scams:

Is a legitimate overseas assignment worth it? An honest assessment

What you might gain

  • Higher real disposable income: besides base pay there are often overseas allowances, housing and transport subsidies, and annual home leave to Taiwan, and the local cost of living is lower than in Taiwan, so you typically save noticeably faster than in a domestic role at the same level
  • Broader responsibilities: overseas offices run lean, so one person covers more ground than the same level at headquarters, and experience builds up faster
  • Cross-border management experience: leading local teams and working with headquarters is a rare credential when moving up

What it costs you

  • Rebuilding your daily life: language, food, healthcare and social life all take readjusting—this is the part most people underestimate
  • Distance from family: Confirm the number and frequency of annual home leave days when negotiating the offer
  • Uncertainty about returning: many people only discover when it's time to come back that their position after returning to Taiwan was never clearly agreed
A headhunter's view

When negotiating an overseas assignment offer, the thing people most often forget to ask, and most often regret, is "the return arrangement": when the three-year posting ends, what position will I have back in Taiwan, and how will my pay carry over?

Almost nobody raises this while excitedly negotiating an offer, but it determines whether the assignment is a plus for your career or a break in it. We recommend asking directly, and ideally getting it into the contract — or at least keeping a written record of the exchange.

FAQ

Are all jobs in Cambodia scams?+

No. Taiwanese companies have real manufacturing and service investments there, and legitimate jobs do exist. But because scam cases are frequent and ongoing (there was a large-scale case as recently as April 2026, and Taiwanese made up most of the foreign nationals arrested), any job offer in Cambodia or Southeast Asia should go through a verification process before you consider it. Don't skip the checks just because the terms look attractive.

How can you tell real from fake in one sentence?+

Check whether it's "screening you." Legitimate overseas assignments are expensive, so employers insist on relevant experience and skills and run a full interview process; scams barely look at your background yet offer high pay. If an opportunity doesn't care what you can do, only whether you can get there, that's a red flag.

What if I'm already abroad and something feels wrong?+

Immediately call the 24-hour emergency line of Taiwan's local representative office, or have family or friends in Taiwan call the Ministry of Foreign Affairs Emergency Contact Center at 0800-085-095. Keep your own identity documents with you if at all possible, stay in regular contact with your family, and keep a record of where you are. Do not sign any debt documents.

How much more does an overseas assignment usually pay than a job in Taiwan?+

There's no fixed multiple; it depends on job level and region. A common structure is "base salary + overseas allowance + housing and transportation subsidies + annual home leave to Taiwan." Combined with the lower local cost of living, real disposable income is usually clearly higher than for an equivalent job in Taiwan. When evaluating an offer, calculate total annual cash, not just the monthly salary figure.

Is applying for overseas jobs through a headhunter safer?+

It's relatively safer, but you still need to verify for yourself. A legitimate recruitment agency must hold an employment services license, may not charge job seekers, and may not hold onto your identity documents. You can ask for their business number and license number and check them yourself. Whatever channel you use, getting a written employment contract before you leave is a step you can't skip.
