Can AI interviews misjudge you? What if you're not good at expressing yourself?

By the Step1ne executive search consultant team Published 2026-09-03 About 3 min
Short answer

Misjudgments can happen, and no one can guarantee they won't. Whether in an AI interview or a human one, the risk exists—that's a limit of hiring itself, not a problem unique to any one tool. What Step1ne does today is add a human checkpoint: A-Cai, our AI interviewer, doesn't make the final call, and every transcript is reviewed by a human consultant before we decide whether to recommend someone to the hiring company. This doesn't resolve the underlying concern that the way AI asks questions may disadvantage some people, but it does ensure no one is screened out by a machine alone without a person ever looking.

This concern isn't imaginary

Potential bias in AI recruiting tools is a real issue that Taiwan's tech media and HR community have been discussing for years; outlets such as Business Next (數位時代) and Manager Today (經理人) have published reporting and analysis on whether AI hiring tools are fair. This isn't something we made up to reassure people. Common concerns include: introverts or people who aren't good at expressing themselves in writing may be at a disadvantage in structured Q&A; people with non-traditional career paths (such as gaps or many career changes) may be misread by standardized questioning logic; and whether AI can truly tell the difference between an answer that is complete and one that is good.

Situations where A-Cai may currently misjudge, listed honestly

  • People who type slowly or aren't strong writers may come across as thin on content. It isn't a lack of ability; written output speed simply doesn't always match how well you express yourself in speech
  • People who give short but genuinely substantive answers may need more follow-up questions to draw it out. A-Cai asks follow-ups on the details you mention, but its follow-up questions aren't guaranteed to be as sharp as a human consultant's
  • Unusual or non-linear career paths take more effort to explain. If your background isn't a straight line, we suggest proactively explaining the reasons behind each turn in your answers, rather than waiting to be asked

To be honest

We won't claim A-Cai is perfectly fair and never misjudges—that isn't true of any interview format, including human interviews. What we can promise is that A-Cai's judgment is not the final word; a human consultant always reads the transcript before deciding. If you're not great at expressing yourself in writing on the spot, or your career path is unusual, we recommend explaining the reasons behind your transitions clearly during the interview rather than assuming the other side—AI or human—will figure them out.

If you worry text-based interviews put you at a disadvantage, what you can do

  • Explain proactively — don't wait to be asked. For unusual experience, employment gaps, or reasons for changing jobs, add the context yourself when a related question comes up instead of waiting for A-Cai to probe
  • Aim for specifics, not polished writing. When consultants review, they look at whether the content has real detail, not whether the sentences are pretty
  • If you felt the conversation didn't go well, you can tell a human consultant directly afterwards. Post-interview feedback and the "Having a problem?" button on screen are both channels that actually get read

FAQ

Will A-Cai judge me poorly because I type slowly?+

There's no strict time limit per answer in the interview — what matters is how complete your answer is, not how fast you type. If you need more time to organize your thoughts, just take it and send your answer once it's complete.

What if I feel A-Cai misunderstood what I meant?+

You can add a clarification right in the conversation, for example: "What I meant just now was..." The transcript is a complete record of the conversation, and when a human consultant reviews it they see the full context, not a single sentence in isolation.

Will AI interviews completely replace human judgment?+

No. A-Cai conducts the structured first-round conversation and produces a transcript, but whether to recommend you to the hiring company is always decided by a human consultant after review. That step is not currently left to automated judgment.
