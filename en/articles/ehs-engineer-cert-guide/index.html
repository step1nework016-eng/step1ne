How do you get an occupational safety and health engineer certificate? What the job actually involves on site

By: Step1ne Executive Search Consultants Published 2026-09-23
Short answer

On site, an occupational safety and health engineer's core job is risk management, not paperwork: running daily toolbox meetings, reviewing contractors' site-entry permits, patrolling the site and managing contractors, and making quick judgment calls and reporting to a supervisor when something goes wrong. On the certification side, the basic threshold is the Class B Occupational Safety and Health Management Technician certificate; workplaces above a certain headcount must also appoint a Class A Occupational Safety and Health Supervisor. These are two separate qualifications and one cannot substitute for the other. In practice, though, certificates are usually a plus rather than a hard requirement when you apply; employers care more about whether you can accept on-site assignments. For new high-tech fab construction projects in places like Huwei, Yunlin (Taiwan), starting pay has risen to NT$50,000–60,000 a month, well above typical construction-site jobs.

What does an occupational safety and health engineer actually do on site?

Going by the title alone, it's easy to assume this is an office job writing reports. In reality, frontline occupational safety and health engineers spend most of the day on the construction site, mainly doing the following:

  • Run the daily "toolbox meeting," reviewing the day's work hazards and precautions with site crews before work begins
  • Review contractors' work permits and site-entry permits to ensure every subcontractor team entering the site meets the rules
  • Prepare occupational safety and health forms and keep records of patrol and audit results
  • Conduct site safety and health patrols and manage contractors entering the site
  • Conduct site surveys and handle tasks assigned by supervisors in line with company project schedules

Take a new high-tech fab project we're working on in Huwei, Yunlin: the engineering department has 10 people, but only 1 safety position. That means this role has to handle occupational safety and health for the entire site on its own, not as part of a team, and hiring managers more often judge candidates on communication, coordination, and on-site judgment than on the certificates themselves.

Are an "Occupational Safety and Health Administrator" and a "Supervisor" the same thing?

No. This is where job seekers most often get confused. "Occupational Safety and Health Administrator" and "Occupational Safety and Health Supervisor" are two different qualification systems, used in different situations:

  • Administrator: corresponds to the "Level B Occupational Safety and Health Management Technician" certificate, part of the skills certification system
  • OSH Supervisor: Comes in Classes A, B, C, and D. Which one is required depends on the employer's workforce size and industry. It belongs to the training system, not the skills certification system

Under current rules, a completion certificate for Occupational Safety and Health Administrator cannot be used directly to serve as a Class A, B, or C Supervisor for the construction industry; each requires separate training or the corresponding qualification. This means that if one person has to carry both the "administrator" and "supervisor" responsibilities, they usually need both certificates and can't use one for both. For the actual rules, refer to the latest announcements from the Occupational Safety and Health Administration of Taiwan's Ministry of Labor; these classifications have changed before and may change again.

How do you get the Class B OSH Administrator certification?

The Class B Occupational Safety and Health Management Technician is a skills certification with two stages: a written exam and a practical exam. There are several common routes to eligibility, such as completing designated occupational safety and health training and obtaining a certificate of completion, or having a degree in a related field; in practice, your eligibility route depends on your own background.

Because eligibility, exam subjects, and exam schedules change from time to time, we recommend checking announcements from the Skills Evaluation Center of the Workforce Development Agency under Taiwan's Ministry of Labor directly, or confirming with a training provider which eligibility category you fall under before registering. Don't rely only on old information online.

What does training for the Class A Occupational Safety and Health Supervisor (construction) certificate cover?

The Class A supervisor credential falls under the training system. Public information indicates about 42 hours of training, covering OSH regulations, an introduction to construction OSH management systems, construction risk assessment, collapse hazard prevention in practice, fall hazard prevention in practice, and safety management of construction machinery — each mapped to high-risk situations that really happen on site, not pure theory.

Whether a Class A supervisor is required depends on the workplace's headcount. A common threshold in the published rules is that a Class A supervisor is required once headcount reaches a certain size (for example, 100 or more employees), while smaller workplaces need Class B or Class C. This training is usually arranged by the employer for current staff; it isn't something job seekers must pass before they can apply. We'll expand on this in the next section.

Without a certification, can I apply first and get certified later?

Yes, and given the actual requirements of this Huwei, Yunlin opening, the answer is even clearer. The listing puts "Class B Occupational Safety and Health Administrator, Construction Class A (or General Industry) Occupational Safety and Health Supervisor, and Class B Occupational Safety and Health Management Technician" under preferred qualifications, stating that "candidates with the above certificates will receive better pay." They are not a prerequisite for applying.

What's actually listed as required is this:

  • Associate degree or above; graduates in occupational safety and health preferred ("preferred," not required)
  • Able to communicate and coordinate with internal teams and subcontractors; a team player
  • Able to relocate to the company's current project sites (currently Huwei, Yunlin)
  • Holds a standard heavy motorcycle license for daily commuting

In other words, companies would rather first find people willing to work on site and able to communicate and coordinate, then keep them on and get them certified through “full subsidies for certification and training,” than lock the door with “certification required before interview.” This matches the logic of other construction openings that welcome career changers from other majors. If you're not yet familiar with the overall entry path, start with How to get into the construction industry: a complete guide for newcomers from other majors.

How does pay for OSH engineers on high-tech fab projects differ from typical site roles?

Semiconductor and high-tech fabs have been expanding rapidly in recent years, and demand for on-site occupational safety and health staff has risen with them, which shows in the pay structure. Taking this role as an example:

  • NT$50,000–60,000 a month (set based on past experience, professional skills, and interview performance, with performance-based raises after that)
  • Pay structure includes a variable base salary, a NT$3,000 meal allowance, and a NT$2,500 safety allowance, plus a supervisory allowance for management roles
  • Staff stationed away from home get employee dormitories (usually near the plant), a monthly trip-home allowance, and a fuel allowance for project assignments
  • Full subsidy for certifications and training, with encouragement to earn the relevant certificates while employed
  • Fixed hours 08:30–17:30, two days off a week; the company says “almost no overtime”

"Almost no overtime" differs from the common impression that site jobs mean "constant overtime." Such differences usually come from how the project is scheduled; we recommend confirming the actual situation directly with the hiring manager in the interview rather than treating the job page description as a guarantee.

FAQ

Can I apply for these roles without an occupational safety and health degree?+

Yes. This role in Huwei, Yunlin lists "junior college or above in an occupational safety and health field" as a plus, not a hard requirement. The actual requirements are communication and coordination skills, willingness to accept site assignments, and a regular heavy motorcycle license; certificates such as the Level B occupational safety and health technician certificate earn a higher salary but aren't a prerequisite for applying.

Can the OSH Administrator and OSH Supervisor certificates substitute for each other?+

No. Under Taiwan's current rules, an occupational safety and health administrator training certificate does not qualify you to serve concurrently as a Class A, B or C construction safety supervisor. They are separate qualification systems, so you need to take the respective training or obtain the matching qualification. For the exact rules, refer to the latest announcements from the Occupational Safety and Health Administration of Taiwan's Ministry of Labor.

What does the Class B Occupational Safety and Health Administrator exam cover?+

It's a type of skills certification with a written and a practical stage. There are several routes to eligibility, such as completing designated occupational safety and health training and obtaining a completion certificate, or holding a degree in a related field. For detailed eligibility, exam subjects, and schedules, please refer to announcements from the Skills Evaluation Center of the Workforce Development Agency, Taiwan Ministry of Labor; these rules are revised from time to time.

Does every construction site need a Class A occupational safety and health supervisor?+

Not every site requires one — it depends on the size of the employer's workforce. A common threshold in published rules is that once a business reaches a certain headcount (e.g., 100 or more employees), it must appoint a Class A supervisor, while smaller businesses correspond to Class B or C. For the actual standards that apply, refer to the latest version of Taiwan's Occupational Safety and Health Management Regulations.

These jobs often require relocating to another city or county. What about my family?+

Take the job in Huwei, Yunlin as an example: the company provides employee dormitories (usually not far from the plant) for staff relocating from other cities and counties, plus a monthly allowance for trips home and a fuel allowance for project assignments. The aim is to lower the cost of living while stationed away from home, and benefits like these are common in project-based jobs at high-tech plants.

How much time and money does certification take?+

This varies by training provider, class, and your individual eligibility. Check the latest fees and hours directly with the Skills Evaluation Center of Taiwan's Workforce Development Agency (Ministry of Labor) or the training provider you plan to enroll with, and don't rely on figures from a single article as your only source.
