How are A-Cai's questions different from a human consultant's?

By the Step1ne executive search consultant team Published 2026-09-03 About 3 min
Short answer

A-Cai doesn't read from a fixed question bank. It follows up based on your résumé, the position you applied for, and details you mention in your answers. It's closer to "asking questions based on your file" than "random chatting." The difference from a human consultant is the division of labor: A-Cai handles a structured first round, clarifying your experience, motivation and hard requirements. Things that need judgment in the moment, such as salary negotiation, career advice and company culture fit, are handled by a human consultant in a confidential follow-up conversation.

What it actually feels like to talk to it

The interview starts by taking a little time to confirm your basic information and explain roughly how long it will take. After that, the questions focus on the experience in your résumé and the position itself, not a random generic question bank. If the job has hard requirements such as an overseas assignment or being stationed at a specific location, these are usually not left until the end, so you won't be caught off guard just as you're wrapping up. When you mention details of your experience, A-Cai will follow up with another question rather than ignoring what you said and jumping to the next one. At the end, it clearly explains who will read the transcript, when you can expect a reply, and how to reach a human consultant.

Sample conversation: how A-Cai follows up on details you mention

The following is a fictional sample conversation to illustrate how A-Cai asks follow-up questions—it is not a transcript of a real interview. Imagine a candidate applying for a customer service manager role who mentions a related experience in passing while describing their work history:

Illustrative scenario · not a real interview record
A-CaiI see. So what are you mainly responsible for in your current job?
Candidate (sample) I mainly led a customer service team of 8, and for a while I also supported a project to improve the returns and exchanges process.
A-CaiCould you tell me more about the return and exchange process improvement? Did you lead it, or were you supporting another department? Roughly what problem did it solve?
Job seeker (example)I led it. Back then a lot of complaints came from return reviews getting stuck on manual cross-checking, so we wrote the review criteria into clear rules, and complaints dropped quite a bit.
A-CaiThat sounds quite relevant to the process-improvement skills this role will need. If you don't mind, roughly how much did complaint volume drop, and how long did it take to see results?

A-Cai won't skip an experience just because it wasn't on the prepared question list; it digs further based on what you share. That also means offering concrete details in your answers usually leads to sharper follow-up questions, not to being ignored.

A headhunter's view

It's clearer when you split it into two layers: handing the structured part to A-Cai means every candidate's first-round record is consistent and comparable; the parts that need judgment in the moment, or adjusting the conversation to someone's reactions, are still done by consultants, and done in more depth, because they no longer spend time asking "what have you done before?" and can pick up from what the transcript has already confirmed. This isn't "AI is better than consultants"; it's splitting up what each does best.

FAQ

Does A-Cai ask questions unrelated to my résumé?+

It won't ask random questions—its questions are based on your résumé and the job you applied for. If a question seems unrelated to the role, you can say so in your answer, or let the consultant know through your feedback after the interview.

Will I be asked about negotiation issues beyond salary?+

A-Cai mainly records your expected salary range; the actual salary negotiation is handled by a human consultant in the confidential first conversation that follows, because it requires on-the-spot judgment about market rates and the role's budget.

For jobs requiring overseas assignment or work at a specific location, when will this come up?+

A-Cai usually confirms these hard requirements early in the conversation rather than saving them for the end. That way, if you really can't meet them, you find out early — and neither side wastes time talking through a whole interview only to discover the terms don't match.
