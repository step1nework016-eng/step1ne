How Do Taiwanese Companies Work with Headhunters? The Full Process from Brief to Start Date

By the Step1ne Executive Search Consultant Team Published 2026-07-27 About 4 min read
Short answer

Most headhunting firms in Taiwan work on a contingency basis, with fees typically 20%–33% of the hire's annual salary, settled after the candidate starts. There are two ways to recruit: retained/commissioned headhunting (proactive outreach to passive candidates) and job-site postings (waiting for applications). Headhunting suits key roles, confidential roles and urgent hires; for general high-volume hiring, you can run both channels in parallel.

When should you use a headhunter instead of just posting on 104?

Job sites wait for people who are "actively job hunting" to apply. But the key talent companies really want is often already employed and not browsing 104; these people can only be reached through a headhunter's direct, confidential approach. If a role meets any of the following, a headhunter is usually more efficient than relying on job sites alone: the position is confidential and can't be advertised publicly; it requires specific rare skills or industry experience; you need to fill many positions in a short time; or you've tried recruiting on your own several times without finding the right person.

What channels are there for working with headhunters in Taiwan?

Engaged executive search (Retained / Contingency Search)

The company retains a headhunting consultant who proactively targets passive candidates in a specific industry and function, approaching and assessing them confidentially. There are two types of mandate: Retained (part of the fee is paid upfront, and the headhunter gives the search priority and deep commitment) and Contingency (the fee is due only when a placement starts work; lower risk for the company, but the headhunter's resources may be less concentrated than under a retained search). Contingency is the norm for small and mid-sized companies and most openings in Taiwan.

Staffing

Instead of hiring directly, the company has a staffing agency (such as Step1ne) sign the employment contract with the worker and cover salary and labor/health insurance, while the company only directs the work on site. This suits flexible staffing, project-based needs, or short-term reinforcements such as for plant construction. Before using staffing, make sure your process is compliant; see The Legal Landmines Companies Most Often Hit with Staffing: Article 17-1 of Taiwan's Labor Standards Act for details.

Fee terms to agree on before working together

Below is a basic explanation of the fee structure. For which terms are actually negotiable and the hidden variables you won't see on a quote, see How do headhunting firms charge? Four things to negotiate beyond the rate.

Most headhunting in Taiwan is success-based: the fee is a percentage of the hired candidate's annual salary, commonly in the 20%-33% range, and the more senior and scarce the role, the higher the percentage tends to be. Fees are usually settled after the candidate starts, and some contracts include a guarantee period (if the hire leaves within a set time after starting, the headhunter provides a replacement free of charge). Before signing, make sure the headhunter puts the fee percentage, the calculation basis (whether bonuses or stock are included), and the guarantee terms in writing to avoid misunderstandings later.

FAQ

What's the difference between a headhunting firm and a job site?+

Job sites are passive: you post an ad, collect résumés broadly, and wait for job seekers to apply. Headhunting is proactive: a consultant targets passive candidates in a specific industry and function (people who are employed and not openly job hunting), and approaches and evaluates them confidentially. It suits key and confidential positions.

Roughly how much do headhunting fees cost?+

The common market range is 20%–33% of the hire's annual salary; the actual rate varies by level, scarcity and firm, and is settled after the candidate starts. We recommend putting the calculation basis and guarantee terms in writing before signing.

Do I pay if no one is successfully placed?+

Most headhunters in Taiwan work on a "success-based" (contingency) model, meaning that in principle there is no fee unless a candidate is placed and starts work; under a retained model, there is an upfront deposit, so be sure to confirm which billing model applies before signing.

How do I confirm a headhunting firm is legitimate?+

Talent service providers in Taiwan must hold an employment services license number issued by the local government. You can simply ask for their business ID and license number, then check on the Ministry of Economic Affairs' business registration public search system that the company is operating normally.
