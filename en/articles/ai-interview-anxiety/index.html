Is an AI interview more nerve-racking than a human one?

By the Step1ne executive search consultant team Published 2026-09-03 About 3 min
Short answer

Not necessarily more nervous, but the source of the nerves is different. With a human interviewer, pressure mostly comes from "they're watching my expressions and my pauses"; with A-Cai that layer disappears, but another one replaces it — "how complete should my typed answers be?" and "did it actually understand me?" Both are real psychological costs. This article breaks them down honestly and doesn't promise that an AI interview is always easier.

Interruptions or distractionsDistractions around you make it awkward, and it's hard to call a stop yourselfJust say it's not a good time, and A-Cai will wait

The following is a fictional demo conversation to illustrate how A-Cai actually responds — it is not a transcript of a real interview. Imagine an applicant for a marketing specialist role interviewing with A-Cai on their phone at home in the evening, when a family member opens the door and comes in, and it suddenly gets a bit noisy:

A-CaiThank you. Next, could you tell me about the marketing planning project you've found most rewarding so far?
Candidate (sample) Hold on, sorry, my family just got home and it's a bit noisy, I might need to first...
A-CaiNo problem — go ahead and take care of that. I'll wait right here, no rush. Just come back and type when you're ready.
Job seeker (example) Okay, sorry to keep you waiting. Where was I?
A-Cai No problem. We were just talking about the project you found most rewarding. You can pick up right where you left off, or I can repeat the question.

A-Cai is designed not to deduct points or rush you if you need to pause midway or there's background noise. If something comes up, it waits for you to deal with it first. That's different from the common worry that "AI is rigid and gets stuck."

A headhunter's view

What we've actually seen: many people are nervous the first time they open the interview room, but about ten minutes in they're often more relaxed than in a human interview, because they know no one on the other side is frowning. The real risk to watch for is the opposite: being so relaxed that your answers become too short or too casual. This interview counts as a formal first-round interview, and the transcript is read by a human consultant, so how complete your answers are still affects how the consultant assesses you. It's not a chat room; no need to be nervous, but don't treat it as idle small talk either.

What if you really get stuck during the interview?

There's always a "Have a question?" button on the interview screen, where you can report common situations directly: A-Cai hasn't responded, you don't understand what a question is asking, it's not a good time and you'd like to reschedule, or you'd rather talk to a human consultant. Tap it and a consultant is notified immediately — no need to wait around or just close the window and give up.

FAQ

Does answering in text make me look less sincere?+

No. How complete your answers are matters more than how fluently you express them. When a human consultant reviews the session, they look at how specific your answers are, not your typing speed or how polished your wording is.

Can I leave partway through and come back later to continue?+

If something comes up and the timing doesn't work, use the "Having a problem?" button on screen and choose "Not a good time, I'd like to reschedule." A consultant will be notified and arrange another time with you.

If I answer wrong or miss a key point, can I add to it?+

Yes—just add the clarification in your next message. Don't worry that once you've sent something you can't change it. The transcript records the whole conversation, and the consultant looks at the full context.
