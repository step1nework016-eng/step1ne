Why Choosing the Wrong Hiring Model Costs More Than You Think

Many companies only think "we need to hire someone" without first working out which hiring model fits the role. The result: for a scarce managerial role that nobody in the market will proactively apply for, they just post on 104 or 1111 (Taiwanese job sites) and wait; or for short-term help needed only for a 3-month project, they hire full-time and then face severance disputes and compensation when the project ends.

Choosing a hiring model is not a gut call; there are three variables you can assess concretely: how long the role is needed, who is responsible for hiring and managing, and how hard the talent is to find. Once these three are clear, whether to use direct-hire recruitment, mid-to-senior executive search, or temporary staffing usually becomes obvious.

FAQ

What is the difference between full-time recruitment, mid-to-senior executive search, and staffing?+

The core difference among the three is who employs the person and for how long. With permanent recruitment and mid-to-senior executive search, the company hires the person directly. The difference is how scarce the talent is: if a general role can be filled through public job postings, use permanent recruitment; for scarce or management roles that require actively approaching passive, currently employed candidates, use mid-to-senior executive search. With temporary staffing, the staffing agency employs the worker and the company only directs on-site work, while the agency carries responsibility for wages, insurance, and labor law compliance.

What happens if you choose the wrong hiring model?+

The most common outcomes are posting a job publicly when you should have used executive search, and failing to find scarce talent that meets your criteria; or hiring someone long-term when a staffing or contract arrangement was the right fit, then running into severance or contract disputes later. The decision should rest on three variables, "how long you need them", "who employs them" and "how hard they are to find", not on gut feeling.

When should you use staffing instead of hiring permanent employees?+

When you want to reduce the administrative burden of hiring (payroll, labor and health insurance, labor law liabilities), or need more staffing flexibility (volatile orders, or unsure about long-term hiring), temporary staffing is a common choice: the staffing agency handles employment and HR management, and the company only manages the on-site work.

When do you need mid-to-senior executive search instead of posting the job yourself?+

When a company needs someone long-term and the role requires specialized skills, management responsibility or scarce talent, a public job posting alone usually fails to reach the right people, because most qualified candidates are already employed and won't apply on their own. That is when you need a headhunter to actively search for and confidentially approach passive candidates who are currently employed.
