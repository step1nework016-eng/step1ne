Construction Project Assistant (Tokyo, Japan / Chinese & Japanese)Accompany architects and contractors to meetings in Tokyo as interpreter, check design and construction progress, and translate construction documents and contracts; full-time, two days off every week

The heart of this role is making sure the Taiwan head office and the local construction team in Japan can understand each other. You will attend and interpret at meetings with Japanese architectural firms and construction companies, confirm planning, design, and construction progress, and coordinate with furniture and interior design vendors for company-owned properties (such as villas). Translating architectural documents, drawings, and contracts makes up the largest share of the work. Before applying, please confirm two things: first, you need at least one year of hands-on experience at an architectural firm or in an architecture-related role; second, you must be able to use both Chinese and Japanese in business settings. An architect's license is not required. The workplace is in Kudanshita, Chiyoda, Tokyo, and you will generally work from the office.

Job description

Meetings and coordination

  • Accompany discussions and meetings with architects and provide Chinese–Japanese interpretation
  • Coordinate with architecture firms and contractors in Japan to move projects forward
  • Coordinating furniture and interior design vendors for company-owned assets (such as villas)

Check status

  • Confirm architectural planning, design, and construction progress

Translation (larger share)

  • Translating architectural documents, drawings, contracts, and other materials

Professional support

  • Use your understanding of Japanese construction practice to fill the team's knowledge gaps and offer professional input

Requirements

Required
  • At least one year of hands-on experience at an architecture firm or in architecture-related work
  • Business-level Chinese
  • Business-level Japanese
  • Strong coordination and communication skills, able to drive work forward independently
Preferred qualifications
  • Experience in architectural project management, design, or construction management
  • Able to serve as a communication bridge between the head office in Taiwan and the local team in Japan
  • Degree in an architecture-related field (not required)
  • English proficiency

Why this opportunity

More than translation

Translation is a large part of the job, but sitting between the architects, the construction company and head office, you will see how an architecture project moves from planning and design through to construction. For anyone aiming for a career in architectural project management, it offers a very complete perspective.

Use your Chinese and Japanese directly in architectural work

The language skills here are used on drawings, contracts and engineering meetings—not general front-desk interpreting. Construction experience plus Chinese and Japanese makes you very hard to replace.

Clear, predictable schedule

Fixed hours of 9:00–18:00, two full days off every week, no early-morning or late-night shifts, and probation terms identical to formal employment.

Cross-border Team

Partners and the hiring team come from different countries and cultural backgrounds, and you will work alongside professional teams in Tokyo and across Japan.

FAQ

Is the first interview conducted by AI?+

Yes. Step1ne's initial interview is an online text conversation with A-Cai, our AI interviewer. It takes about 20 to 30 minutes, at a time you choose, with no need to take time off, and it counts as the formal first-round interview. Afterward, a human consultant reviews it and contacts you; your information is never sent to an employer without your consent. Only after passing do you move on to the employer's interviews.

What exactly is the pay?+

Salary is negotiable based on experience; a consultant will explain the actual range and breakdown during the initial phone conversation. There is also a pay-raise system, and bonuses depend on company performance.

What level of Japanese do I need?+

Business-level proficiency is required: enough to interpret in meetings with architects and construction companies and to translate architectural documents, drawings, and contracts. Your consultant will check with the employer whether a test score is needed.

Can I apply if I did not study architecture or a related field?+

Yes. An architecture-related degree isn't required, but you do need at least one year of hands-on experience at an architectural firm or in architecture-related work. An architect's license isn't required either.

I'm in Taiwan. What about a work visa and residence status?+

The source information does not mention residence status or who handles the visa, nor whether candidates still living in Taiwan are accepted, so this page does not give an answer in advance. Your consultant is checking with the employer and will reply to you directly once confirmed.
